0. The three edits used, and why three
--------------------------------------

  editbody   `bench/devloop_bench.sh`'s self.editbody: append
             `proc devloopBenchBody(): int = 1` to sem.nim.  This is the edit
             the phase's gate is stated on.  The proc is PRIVATE and never
             called, so `dce` removes it: nothing downstream of hexer sees it.
             It also mints no temp and no hook.

  live       one statement in an existing, exported, heavily called proc
             (`semStmt`), changed IN PLACE and keeping the token count:
             `if dest.len == <k>: quit 1` with `<k>` renumbered.  Survives DCE.

  grow       the same statement ADDED to `semStmt`, so the module gains one
             statement and one xelim temp.  This is what a developer's edit
             normally is, and it is the only one of the three that moves
             hexer's module-wide counters.

`editbody` alone is not a representative measurement, and section 2 is the
reason the other two exist.

3. The premise of step 1, measured on the F1 sidecar
----------------------------------------------------

`<mod>.decls.nif` records, per named top-level declaration, a line-info-blind
digest of its sem INPUT and one of its lowering OUTPUT.  That is exactly the
pair a declaration-level hexer consults: "did this declaration's input
change" decides what to re-lower, "did its output change" decides what may be
spliced from the previous `.x.nif`.  sem has 1794 of them.

  edit       sem-input digest changed   lowering-output digest changed
  editbody        0  (+1 added)               0  (+1 added)
  live            1  of 1794                  3  of 1794
  grow            1  of 1794                890  of 1794

Reproduced twice, identical both times.

Cross-check, and a caveat for whoever re-measures.  The textual method the
`decl-stability` scenario uses (`splitNifDecls`/`diffDecls`, split the root
`(stmts ...)` into children and match by name) agrees with the sidecar on the
`grow` edit -- 896 blind vs 890 -- but it OVERSTATES churn on any edit that
adds a child, and the two must not be confused:

  sem .x.nif: 3550 children, of which 1762 carry a `:SymbolDef` and
              1788 are anonymous (1268 `(x`, 494 `(h`, ...)

`declNameOf` collapses an anonymous child onto its TAG, and `diffDecls`
matches repeated names positionally within that group, so ONE inserted
anonymous node shifts the whole group and every later member reads as
"changed".  That is why the same `editbody` edit reads 0 changed in the
sidecar (which is keyed by symbol) and 945 in a textual `.x.nif` split.  The
sidecar is the number to trust, and this is a concrete reason F1 was right to
build it rather than diff bytes.

F1 did its job: nimsem's output is declaration-local under all three edits.
HEXER'S IS NOT.  One changed input declaration becomes 890 changed output
declarations -- half the module -- as soon as the edit mints one extra temp.

The same thing, seen at the next two artifacts (`grow`, blind = NIF line-info
tokens stripped):

  sem .c.nif  : 1974 decls | changed 638 (blind 420) added 11 removed 11
  sem .asm.nif: 1949 decls | changed 467 (blind 467) added  0 removed  0

and for the `live` edit, which mints no temp:

  sem .c.nif  : 1974 decls | changed 1 | sem .asm.nif: 1949 decls | changed 1


4. The cause, isolated
----------------------

Diff of `semGetSize`, a proc the edit does not touch, in sem's `.c.nif`
before and after `grow` (line info stripped):

    -   (var :`x.840 . xint.0.xincut4p51
    +   (var :`x.841 . xint.0.xincut4p51
    -   (asgn result.0`semGetSize`0 `x.840)
    +   (asgn result.0`semGetSize`0 `x.841)

and of `sameIdent`:

    -    (var :t.0h65 .          +    (var :t.0h91 .
    -     (var :idx.0h68 .       +     (var :idx.0h94 .
    -       (var :`x.0h69 .      +       (var :`x.0h95 .
    -        (var :s.0h72 .      +        (var :s.0h98 .
    -         (lab :returnLabel.0h74)

Two module-wide counters, both named in `notes/b3b.md` section 2:

  `x.<n>`  -- `xelim.Context.counter`, carried across the pipeline as
              `Pass.nextTemp` (`src/hexer/xelim.nim:102,635,1223,1521,1531`,
              `src/hexer/passes.nim:21`)
  `0h<n>`  -- `intramodinliner.InlinerCtx.counter`
              (`src/hexer/intramodinliner.nim:400-417,1095-1097`)

One extra temp in the edited proc shifts every later `x.N in the module by
one, and the extra inlining it causes shifts the inliner's counter by 26.


5. The prefix theorem is FALSE, and this is the important part
--------------------------------------------------------------

`notes/b3b.md` section 2 offered a fallback that needed no counter
checkpointing at all:

  "since every pass processes declarations in order, if declarations 1..k are
   unchanged then after every pass the output prefix for 1..k is identical and
   every counter is at the same value.  A prefix-preserving incremental hexer
   is therefore sound without any counter checkpointing."

It is not sound.  On `tests/incremental/b3b_lib.nim` (13 procs, `step1` first,
`step10` last), adding `if s.len == 99 and acc > 0: acc = acc + 1` -- an `and`,
which xelim binds to a bool temp -- to `step10`, the LAST declaration:

  decls 23 | sem-input changed 1 | lowering-output changed 11 | added 0
  changed: step1 step2 step3 step4 step5 step6 step7 step8 step9 step10 total

and the diff of `step1`, the FIRST declaration of the module:

    -   (var :`x.0 .     +   (var :`x.1 .
    -     (add (i 64)acc.0`step1`0 `x.0))
    +     (add (i 64)acc.0`step1`0 `x.1))

An edit at the END of the module renumbers a temp at the BEGINNING.  The
reason is that `pipeline.transform` runs eleven passes over the whole buffer
and `lowerExprs` runs three of them (`xelim1`, `xelim_finalir`,
`xelim_final`) sharing one monotone counter: the temps `step1` receives on
the SECOND run depend on how many the FIRST run minted in `step10`.  The
prefix theorem holds WITHIN one pass and fails for the pipeline.

Consequence: there is no cheap sound splice.  Either the counters are scoped
per declaration first, or every one of them (seven, `notes/b3b.md` section 2)
plus `EContext.hoistedConsts`, `c.pending`, `c.strLits`, `c.newTypes`,
`c.initBody` and `LiftingCtx`'s two hook tables has to be checkpointed and
restored per declaration, in both directions.  A mistake in any of them is
two declarations minting the same `x.N -- one `SymId` by
`pool.syms.getOrIncl`, i.e. a miscompile, not a slow path.


6. arkham and nifasm, measured before touching either
-----------------------------------------------------

The brief asks whether unchanged procs' asm-NIF is byte-identical after a body
edit, and cites `notes/b3b.md` section 5's 135-of-974 "pure drift" (asm
changed although the `.c.nif` declaration did not).

  edit    .c.nif changed   .asm.nif changed   pure drift
  live          1                 1                0
  grow        638               467               28   of 1949

Pure arkham drift is 28 declarations, and 0 when the edit does not move token
positions.  The 467 changed asm declarations are inherited from the 420
genuinely changed Leng declarations of section 3 -- i.e. from hexer's
counters, not from arkham's.  F1 removed most of the drift the earlier note
measured, because that drift was downstream of sem's module-wide local
renumbering shifting the token positions arkham names its temps from.

nifasm's own numbers for the whole-image link (NIFASM_PROFILE=1), identical
for `live` and `grow`:

  blobHits 3415   blobStale 18   blobRecorded 627
  emitRoots 296-305 ms of a 324-335 ms link
  per-module emit, top 2:  semygjvq21 187.8 ms / 867 syms
                           semv1g3zm   24.2 ms / 107 syms

18 stale, not the 540 the brief expected -- B3c already narrowed that.  What
is left is `blobRecorded 627`: the cache's unit is the symbol but its validity
check is per MODULE.  `blobcache.sameSource` (src/nifasm/blobcache.nim:449 at
the pin c3f27fc) compares the module file's stamp and, failing that, a hash of
the whole file; :752 applies it to the fragment's own module and :760 to every
module the fragment depends on.  A two-line change to sem's `.asm.nif`
therefore drops every fragment whose symbol lives in sem, and every fragment
elsewhere that references one.
Per-symbol validity would take 627 recorded down to

    ~467 today          (saving ~55 ms of a 1.28 s loop, ~4 %)
    ~2   once hexer's counters are scoped   (saving ~180 ms, ~14 %)

which is why it was not built now: its value is gated on a change that is not
in this phase's owner set of files, and landing it first buys 4 %.


7. Verdict per step of the brief
--------------------------------

  step 1  declaration-level incremental hexer
          NOT BUILT.  Floor 112 ms against a 100 ms gate even if every
          decomposable stage went to zero (section 2), and its splice is
          unsound today because 890 of 1794 unchanged-input declarations get
          different lowering output (section 3), in both directions along the
          module (section 5).  A version that fell back to full re-lowering
          whenever a temp count moved would fall back on essentially every
          real edit.

  step 2  arkham per-symbol stability
          NOT BUILT.  Its own premise measures at 28 of 1949 declarations
          (section 6).  Scoping arkham's labels/rodata/temps costs every
          `.asm.nif` golden in nativenif and a non-byte-identical refactor
          gate, and fixes 28 of the 467 that actually change.

  step 3  nifasm per-symbol blob validity
          NOT BUILT.  Real (627 recorded vs 467 changed) but worth ~4 % until
          step 0 below lands, and ~14 % after it.

  step 0  (new, and the measurement's own recommendation)
          scope `Pass.nextTemp` and `InlinerCtx.counter` -- and then the other
          five of `notes/b3b.md` section 2 -- per top-level declaration, the
          way F1 scoped `sembasics.makeLocalSym` per routine.  Nothing in
          steps 1-3 pays before it.
