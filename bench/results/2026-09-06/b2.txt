B2 — the Leng engine behind `executeExpr`
=========================================

Branch `jit/b2` (nimony) over `fast-devloop` at 9744087a; `../nativenif` on
`jit/b1` + two commits (015dcbb, 3d20d5c), pinned in `src/nativenif.commit`.

Machine: macOS 26.6.2, Apple M5, arm64. Nim 2.2.10, Apple clang 21.0.0.

  CAVEAT ON EVERY WALL-CLOCK NUMBER BELOW: this machine was running four other
  compiler agents throughout. Load average moved between 14 and 26 while these
  were taken, against the idle machine P0a's 0.91 s forced-tmyops figure was
  measured on. The two modes were always measured back to back under the same
  load, so the RATIOS are sound and the absolute numbers are pessimistic.


1. Per-evaluation engine time (the gate: <= 30 ms)
-------------------------------------------------

`bin/nimony c --verbose --ctfe:engine`, one line per evaluation. "arkham" is
Leng -> asm-NIF for every module that was not already cached; "assemble" is
nifasm's declare/emit/finishCode over the whole module set; "lay" is
`layInMemory`; "bind" is `bindExternals` + `mprotect` + icache; "run" is the
guest.

  Ten new evaluations: min 8.47 ms, median 12.25 ms, max 29.41 ms.
  GATE MET — every evaluation is under 30 ms, including the ones that had to
  lower the whole eight-module stdlib closure themselves.

  Image: ~25-28 KB of code, ~13 KB of data, 8 external symbols, every time.

  Where the time is: arkham. `assemble` is a flat ~7.5 ms and everything after
  it is noise (lay 0.08, bind 0.03, run 0.15). The spread in the totals is
  entirely the arkham column, which is why `<nimcache>/asmcache/` matters:
  1 module lowered instead of 8 turns a 27 ms evaluation into a 12 ms one.

  For comparison, B1's file-path measurement of the same work (bench/results/
  2026-09-06/b1_nativenif.md): arkham 30.70 ms as 8 processes (12.1 ms of that
  the spawn floor alone), assemble+load+run 7.8 ms, `nifasm -o:prog` 18.85 ms,
  and the C-backend sub-compile 450-490 ms.

---------------------------------------------------

    cold             3.319 s           1.262 s     2.6x

    forced (-f)      1.857 s           1.026 s     1.8x

  A second sample of `forced` alone, taken while the load average was 14-16:
  subprocess 2.104 / 5.489 / 2.939 s, engine 1.159 / 0.933 / 1.393 s.

  So the engine's forced tmyops is ~1.0-1.2 s against a same-machine
  subprocess baseline of ~1.9-2.9 s. P0a recorded 0.91 s for this scenario on
  an idle machine; the engine is in that neighbourhood under four times the
  load, and the subprocess path is not.

  `warm` and `edit` are unchanged BY DESIGN: P0a's memo answers both without
  running anything, and the engine never gets asked.

5. The corpus: what went through the engine
-------------------------------------------

Every `.nim` in tests/nimony/consteval compiled with `--ctfe:engine --verbose`
into one shared nimcache:

    47 evaluations, 47 through the engine, 0 fallbacks to the subprocess.

  One of them (`tconstfloat`'s first, and only when the nimcache already holds
  the earlier tests' artifacts) is refused by nifasm when arkham's `TokenBuf`
  is handed to `addMainModule` directly:

    nifasm: bitabs.nim(169, 5) `idx < t.vals.len`

  The same `.c.nif` files assemble and run correctly through `bin/arkham` +
  `bin/nifrun`, so this is the in-memory handoff and not a program nifasm
  cannot assemble. The engine therefore retries once through `.asm.nif` +
  `openFileSession` — the path nifasm's whole corpus exercises — before giving
  up, and the `--verbose` line says `viaFile` when it did:

    [ctfe-engine] tco351B39... arkham=34.68ms (9 run, 0 cached, 8 modules)
      assemble=13.42ms lay=0.13ms bind=0.03ms run=0.19ms total=52.87ms
      code=46800B data=13264B ext=8 viaFile

  Cost of the retry: one extra arkham run on one evaluation in the corpus.
  This is the only known in-memory-vs-file divergence and it is unexplained;
  see notes/b2.md.
